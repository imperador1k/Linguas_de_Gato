# Vocabulary pilot — human review protocol

How to review the pilot proposal produced by the vocabulary data pipeline (`tools/vocabulary-data`). The proposal holds **400 English lexical candidates** (target language `en`), selected from redistributable sources (Google Books top lists, CC BY 3.0; Open English WordNet 2025, CC BY 4.0).

The protocol's principles carry over to other languages; its English-specific checks do not (see `docs/VOCABULARY_CONTENT_MODEL.md` §7–8). Those checks include British/American variants, the missing function words in Open English WordNet, and "'s" possessives.

It is **not**:
- a learner catalogue;
- an A1 curriculum;
- a list of 400 final learning entries.

The content unit after review is **lemma + part of speech + sense**. One candidate spelling may become several entries, or none.

The review package is generated by:

```bash
python -m vocabulary_data review-package --pilot pilot-v3-a --run final-c --out pilot-v3-review
```

It lands in `tools/vocabulary-data/output/redistributable/pilot-v3-review/`, which is git-ignored and contains:

| File | Content |
| --- | --- |
| `review-all.csv` | Every candidate, once. |
| `review-batch-1.csv` … `review-batch-8.csv` | The same 400 rows, 50 per batch, dealt round-robin so each batch spans every group and rank band. |
| `COLUMNS.md` | Every column explained. |
| `pilot-overview.md` | Composition, ambiguity, examples for reviewers. |
| `function-word-coverage.md` / `.csv` | How the approved sources cover function words. |
| `ATTRIBUTION.md`, `review-manifest.json` | Sources and checksums. |

Reviewers fill only the review columns. Provenance columns are never edited.

## 1. Decisions and what happens next

`reviewer_decision` takes one of the values below. A decision **never** turns a candidate into an app pack entry by itself. A later, separate step (not built yet) will validate the reviewed CSV and write a list of approved lexical entries; enrichment and pack generation come after that, each with its own review.

| Decision | Use when | Effect on later processing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `accept` | The spelling is a valid lexical item, the proposed lemma and parts of speech are right, and one entry is enough. | One approved entry (lemma + part of speech), queued for enrichment. |
| `accept_with_edits` | Valid, but the lemma, parts of speech or senses need correcting. | As accept, using `approved_lemma`, `approved_parts_of_speech`, `approved_senses`. The reason goes in `lexical_review_notes`. |
| `split_by_pos` | The spelling is genuinely several words by part of speech ("book" noun / verb). | One approved entry per value in `approved_parts_of_speech`. Each is enriched and reviewed separately. |
| `split_senses` | One part of speech, but meanings a learner must keep apart ("bank" of a river / for money). | One entry, with one sense per item in `approved_senses`. Each sense gets its own translations, definitions, examples and review status. |
| `merge_into` | The candidate is a form or spelling variant of another entry ("analyse" → "analyze"; an irregular form). | No separate entry. It is recorded as a form or variant of the entry named in `approved_lemma`. |
| `reject` | Not a learner lexical item: invalid, a name or artefact, or too obscure for the catalogue. | Never enriched. The record and the reason stay for traceability. |
| `defer` | The evidence is insufficient, or another source or decision is needed (e.g. function-word roles). | Stays pending. Revisit when the blocker is resolved. |

## 2. Lexical review

Work from the evidence in the row. Never confirm a lemma from a suffix alone: "-s", "-ed" and "-ing" endings are not evidence.

1. **Is it a valid English lexical item?** Check `lemma_evidence` and `source_ids`. Watch for:
   - names and acronyms (`quality_flags`: `acronym_likely`, `capitalization_ambiguous`, `capitalized_ordinary_word`);
   - abbreviations and OCR debris.

   If it is a name or artefact, reject it.
2. **Is the proposed dictionary form correct?** `proposed_lemma` is empty when the identity is ambiguous; `lemma_evidence` lists every reading with its source. For a form of another word (`explicit_form`), decide between `merge_into` and keeping it as a word in its own right (e.g. "shot" as a noun). Irregular forms already attached appear in `morphology_evidence`.
3. **Is the part of speech supported?** `part_of_speech` comes from Open English WordNet, which has **no** prepositions, determiners, pronouns, conjunctions or modal roles. Function words in the pilot (by, above, no) therefore show only other parts of speech. Record the missing role in `lexical_review_notes`, and defer if unsure.
4. **Do different parts of speech need separate entries?** If a learner would use them as different words, use `split_by_pos`. If they share one meaning and exercise, use `accept`.
5. **Should senses be distinguished?** Only for meanings a learner must keep apart. List short glosses in `approved_senses`; definitions are written later, not here.
6. **Spelling variant or a different word?** `spelling_variant_information` comes from spelling markers on a shared meaning, and can be sense-specific (storey/story only for the floor of a building). Variants are linked, never merged automatically. Decide which spelling is the entry and use `merge_into` for the other, or keep both with a note.
7. **Valid but too obscure?** Use `reject` with the reason "obscure", or `defer` if it could belong to a specialist pack later. Many deep-band candidates (rank > 12,000) are book-heavy: pleural, putative, sociocultural.
8. **Is the evidence sufficient?** If it isn't, defer, and say what is missing.
9. **Reject or defer?** Reject when the item is clearly not wanted. Defer when the answer depends on evidence or decisions not yet available. Record uncertainty instead of guessing.

## 3. Pedagogical review

The app serves learners from Pre-A1 to C2. The pilot was selected for **lexical** review using frequency in printed books. It must not become the beginner curriculum automatically. The Pre-A1/A1 curriculum will be designed separately, around communication goals, grammar progression, vocabulary and learner tasks.

- **`pedagogical_usefulness`:**
  - `essential_everyday`: needed in ordinary daily life;
  - `useful_general`;
  - `context_specific`: useful in some domains or situations;
  - `specialist`;
  - `low_priority_or_obscure`.
- **`beginner_relevance`:** a preliminary human judgement, **not** a CEFR level.
  - `core`: a beginner needs it early;
  - `useful`;
  - `not_beginner_priority`;
  - `uncertain`.
- **`recommended_learning_priority`:** free text or a reviewer scale.

Never infer it from the rank: a high book rank is not a beginner priority ("ratification"), and many beginner words rank low in books ("fridge").

## 4. Function-word coverage

`function-word-coverage.md` checks a hand-authored checklist of articles, pronouns, determiners, prepositions, conjunctions, auxiliaries, modals, contractions and other very frequent words against the redistributable data. The checklist is a review aid only.

What it shows (pilot v3):
- **Articles:** "the" has no dictionary form in the approved lexicon. "a" and "an" match only the letter/noun readings.
- **Pronouns:** 38 of 49 have no dictionary form (me, my, you, they, which, what); 11 match other words (it → IT, he → helium).
- **Determiners, prepositions, conjunctions:** mostly present only with other parts of speech (some: adjective; by: adverb; but: adverb). Many have no dictionary form at all (of, to, with, from, and, because, if).
- **Auxiliaries:**
  - **be, have, do:** exist only as lexical verbs.
  - **Their forms:**
    - attached as explicit forms of be/have/do: is, was, were, been, has, had, did;
    - ambiguous: am, are, being, done;
    - unsupported: having, does.
- **Modals:** could, should, would, shall and ought are unsupported; can, may, might, must and will match only other words or the lexical verb.
- **Contractions:** absent. The Google lists split contractions into separate tokens; only "don't" survives, and it is unsupported.

Nothing is added to the pilot automatically. These words matter most for beginners, but the approved sources cannot support them reliably. Sources worth **researching** (none adopted; each needs licence verification from its primary source and a decision):
- **An editorial closed-class list owned by the project.** Function words are a small, closed set (a few hundred items), and the most practical route may be a curated, reviewed list with the project as author. That is an editorial decision, not a third-party source.
- **The English Speller Database.** It already gives parts of speech for function words, but it is restricted until its author clarifies the licence for non-wordlist results (see `tools/vocabulary-data/README.md`).
- **Universal Dependencies English treebanks.** Part-of-speech-tagged corpora; the licence is reported as share-alike and must be verified.
- **Wiktionary.** Part-of-speech sections for function words; CC BY-SA / GFDL, so share-alike.
- **Contractions** could be described editorially as forms of their bases (don't = do + not) rather than taken from a frequency list.

## 5. The restricted cross-check

The pipeline can cross-check the pilot against restricted sources. Its latest run flagged 121 entries:
- missing parts of speech;
- rare in everyday use;
- also a form of another word.

It lives only in `output/restricted/` and is marked restricted.

Rules:
- It must not appear in review CSVs, the pilot overview or app packs.
- It must not alter the redistributable source register.
- It is never automatic proof for including or excluding a candidate.
- A reviewer may use it as a prompt to look harder, and must base the recorded decision on redistributable evidence and their own judgement.

## 6. Preserving uncertainty

- **Empty means unknown:** an empty `proposed_lemma` or a `part_of_speech` of `unknown` means unknown, not "none".
- **Flags are prompts, not verdicts.** "No flags" does not mean verified.
- **When in doubt, defer** and write what would settle it.
- **Keep the provenance columns untouched,** so every decision stays traceable to the pilot, the run and the sources.
