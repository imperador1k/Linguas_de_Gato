# Learning framework: Pre-A1 to C2

Status: **proposal** (M10, 22 November 2026). Nothing here is implemented. It defines what the app should teach and how progress is judged, as the basis for `docs/ROADMAP_NEXT_PHASES.md`. The evidence about the current app is in `docs/PRODUCT_AUDIT_PRE_A1_C2.md`.

Scope: English as the target language and Portuguese (Portugal) as the support language. The structure is meant to work for other languages; the content is not.

## 1. Principles

1. **CEFR describes what a learner can do with the language, not word lists.** Levels are defined by communicative tasks ("can introduce themselves and ask simple personal questions"), with the conditions under which they are done: support, speed, topic familiarity, accuracy. Frequency ranks, vocabulary sizes and grammar inventories are *ingredients*, never the definition of a level.
2. **Skills are levelled separately.** A learner might read at B1 and speak at A2. The profile keeps one level per skill:
   - listening;
   - reading;
   - spoken interaction;
   - spoken production;
   - writing.

   Mediation, which the CEFR Companion Volume (2020) adds, is out of scope for now.
3. **Receptive before productive, but not only receptive.** A word or structure is first met in meaningful input, then recognised, then recalled, then used. Each stage is a separate state, never assumed from the previous one.
4. **Teach, then practise, then use.** New language is presented with meaning, sound and examples before the learner is asked to produce it. Testing is not teaching.
5. **Comprehensible input.** Graded texts and audio where the learner knows nearly all the words. Research on reading suggests about 98% known running words for comfortable unassisted reading, and 95% as a minimum with support (Hu & Nation, 2000; Laufer & Ravenhorst-Kalovski, 2010).
6. **The support language is a tool that fades.** At Pre-A1/A1, instructions, grammar explanations and feedback are in Portuguese. English replaces them progressively, and by B1 the interface can be English by choice.
7. **Evidence of learning over activity.** Progress is shown as what the learner can now do and retain, not minutes or actions (§6).
8. **The app estimates; it does not certify.** Levels shown are estimates from evidence the app holds, always with that evidence visible. No claim of an official CEFR level.

## 2. The learner profile

| Field | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Support language | `pt-PT` today. The language of explanations and translations. |
| Interface language | `pt-PT` or `en`; independent of the support language. |
| Skill levels | Listening, reading, spoken interaction, spoken production, writing. Each has an estimate (Pre-A1 … C2) with its evidence and date. |
| Goals | E.g. travel, work, exams, conversation. Influences topics, not levels. |
| Current path position | The unit and lesson in progress. |
| Item knowledge | Per word sense, chunk and grammar point, a state for each direction (§6.2). |

**Placement:**
- **Beginner route:** "I have never studied English" starts at Pre-A1 with no test.
- **Other learners:**
  - self-assessment with can-do statements in Portuguese, per skill;
  - followed by short checks in the skills the learner can be tested on automatically (listening, reading, vocabulary, grammar recognition);
  - speaking and writing start from self-assessment and are revised from the first tasks.
- **Re-placement:** placement is cheap to repeat and can always be overridden by the learner.

## 3. Levels

The vocabulary sizes below are **indicative research estimates of receptive vocabulary**, not CEFR requirements. The often-cited figures (Milton, 2010, from specific learner populations and tests) are:
- A1: under about 1,500 words;
- A2: about 1,500–2,500;
- B1: about 2,750–3,250;
- B2: about 3,250–3,750;
- C1: about 3,750–4,500;
- C2: about 4,500–5,000.

Productive vocabulary is considerably smaller. Use these for planning content volume, never for placing a learner.

Grammar scope follows the broad consensus of published inventories, such as the British Council–EAQUALS Core Inventory for General English and the English Grammar Profile. These are references for editorial judgement; their data is not used without a licence check.

### Pre-A1
- **Communicative objectives:** recognise and use isolated words and set phrases for immediate needs:
  - greetings and goodbyes;
  - their own name and spelling it;
  - numbers;
  - "yes/no/please/thank you/sorry";
  - understanding the app's own instructions.
- **Vocabulary:** about 100–200 items:
  - classroom and app instruction words;
  - numbers 0–100;
  - colours;
  - days;
  - family members;
  - everyday objects;
  - set chunks ("My name is…", "I don't understand", "Can you repeat, please?").
- **Grammar:** no explicit grammar beyond chunks; `be` in fixed phrases (I'm…, It's…); the alphabet and spelling.
- **Listening:** single words and short phrases, slowly and clearly, with pictures, repeated as needed; recognise numbers and names.
- **Speaking:** repeat words and phrases; say name, age and country with a model.
- **Reading:** single words and familiar phrases with pictures; signs.
- **Writing:** copy words; write name, numbers and simple personal details in a form.
- **Exercises:**
  - picture–word and sound–word matching;
  - listen and choose;
  - listen and repeat;
  - spell your name;
  - copy;
  - number dictation.
- **Progression:**
  - recognises (listening and reading) at least 85% of the stage's core items, at least a week after first meeting them;
  - completes the "introduce yourself" checkpoint with a model.

### A1
- **Communicative objectives:**
  - introduce themselves and others;
  - ask and answer simple personal questions;
  - talk about family, home, routines, likes;
  - buy things and ask prices;
  - tell the time;
  - follow simple instructions and directions;
  - all with a cooperative, slow speaker.
- **Vocabulary:** about 500–800 items for everyday topics (personal details, family, home, food, shopping, time, daily routine, places in town, weather, free time) plus the core closed-class words (pronouns, articles, basic prepositions, conjunctions and, but, because).
- **Grammar:**
  - `be` (all forms, questions, negatives);
  - present simple with `do/does`;
  - `have` / `have got`;
  - `can` for ability and requests;
  - `there is/are`;
  - a/an/the (basic) and plurals;
  - this/that/these/those;
  - possessive adjectives and 's;
  - subject and object pronouns;
  - imperatives;
  - prepositions of place and time (in/on/at, basic);
  - question words;
  - basic word order (adjective before noun);
  - `like` + noun or -ing;
  - present continuous for now (late A1);
  - `was/were` (late A1, bridging to A2).
- **Listening:** short, slow, clearly articulated dialogues on familiar topics; understand numbers, prices, times and key information.
- **Speaking:**
  - **Interaction:** simple questions and answers, with repetition and help.
  - **Production:** a few linked sentences about themselves.
- **Reading:** very short simple texts (messages, notices, profiles, menus), finding familiar names, words and basic phrases.
- **Writing:** short simple messages and forms; simple sentences linked with and/but/because.
- **Exercises:** all Pre-A1 types, plus:
  - multiple-choice gap fill;
  - word order (build the sentence);
  - question–answer matching;
  - short dialogue completion;
  - guided role-play (choose, then say);
  - sentence dictation;
  - short guided writing with a model.
- **Progression:** A1 can-do checkpoints passed in each skill:
  - **Listening and reading:** at least 80% on unseen texts at level.
  - **Speaking and writing:** checkpoint tasks meet the descriptor checklist.
  - **Vocabulary:** at least 80% of A1 core items recalled after at least a week.
  - **Per skill:** each skill can advance separately.

### A2
- **Communicative objectives:**
  - routine exchanges on familiar topics;
  - describe past events and experiences simply;
  - make plans and arrangements;
  - describe people, places and things;
  - compare;
  - handle shopping, travel and services;
  - write short personal messages.
- **Vocabulary:** about 1,500–2,500 receptive. Topics broaden (travel, health, work, education, feelings). First common phrasal verbs (get up, look for) and collocations (make a mistake, do homework).
- **Grammar:**
  - past simple (regular and irregular, questions, negatives);
  - past continuous (basic);
  - `going to` and present continuous for future;
  - `will` (basic);
  - comparatives and superlatives;
  - countable/uncountable with some/any/much/many/a lot of;
  - adverbs of manner and frequency;
  - `have to`, `should`, `must` (basic);
  - present perfect (experience: ever/never; introduced);
  - object pronouns and possessive pronouns;
  - `too/enough`;
  - first conditional (introduced);
  - linkers so/then/after that.
- **Listening:** short clear recordings on everyday topics (announcements, simple conversations, instructions) at near-normal speed with clear articulation.
- **Speaking:**
  - **Interaction:** short social exchanges.
  - **Production:** describe routines, past events and plans in simple connected sentences.
- **Reading:** short everyday texts (emails, adverts, timetables, simple stories), finding specific predictable information.
- **Writing:** short notes, emails and simple descriptions or narratives with basic linkers.
- **Exercises:**
  - typed gap fill;
  - word forms;
  - transformation (simple);
  - dialogue role-play;
  - listening for gist and detail;
  - graded reading with questions;
  - short writing with feedback;
  - picture description (spoken).
- **Progression:** as A1, with A2 can-do checkpoints and A2 core vocabulary.

### B1
- **Communicative objectives:**
  - deal with most travel situations;
  - talk about experiences, events, hopes and plans with reasons and explanations;
  - follow the main points of clear standard input on familiar matters;
  - write simple connected text;
  - narrate;
  - give opinions.
- **Vocabulary:** about 2,750–3,250 receptive. Abstract and work-related vocabulary, more phrasal verbs and collocations, word families (decide/decision).
- **Grammar:**
  - present perfect (contrast with past simple; for/since);
  - present perfect continuous (introduced);
  - past perfect;
  - `used to`;
  - second conditional;
  - passive (present and past simple);
  - relative clauses (defining);
  - reported speech (statements, basic);
  - modals of possibility and deduction (might, may, could, must, can't);
  - gerund vs infinitive (common verbs);
  - future forms contrasted;
  - linkers (although, however, because of).
- **Listening:** main points of clear standard speech on familiar topics: radio or podcasts at moderate speed; longer conversations.
- **Speaking:** sustain a simple conversation, narrate, describe, give and justify opinions briefly; handle unpredictable everyday situations.
- **Reading:** straightforward factual texts and narratives; understand the main points, the writer's opinion (simple), and descriptions of events and feelings.
- **Writing:** connected texts (emails, short essays, stories, reviews) on familiar topics.
- **Exercises:**
  - sentence production with target words;
  - error correction;
  - sense in context;
  - extended listening with note-taking;
  - longer graded reading;
  - writing with AI feedback (errors versus suggestions);
  - spoken monologue with feedback.
- **Progression:** B1 can-do checkpoints. The English interface is offered by default.

### B2
This is where the app is strongest today.
- **Communicative objectives:**
  - interact with fluency and spontaneity with native speakers;
  - produce clear, detailed text;
  - argue a viewpoint with advantages and disadvantages;
  - understand the main ideas of complex texts, including technical discussion in their field.
- **Vocabulary:** about 3,250–3,750 receptive. Precision, collocation, register awareness, idiomatic phrasal verbs.
- **Grammar:**
  - all tenses including future perfect and continuous;
  - third and mixed conditionals;
  - wish / if only;
  - passive (all forms; have something done);
  - reported speech (questions, commands, reporting verbs);
  - relative clauses (non-defining, with prepositions);
  - participle clauses (introduced);
  - modals of past deduction;
  - linking and discourse markers.
- **Listening:** extended speech and lectures, news and films in standard language.
- **Speaking:** fluent discussion, explaining viewpoints, presenting.
- **Reading:** articles and reports on contemporary problems; contemporary prose.
- **Writing:** essays and reports that argue and evaluate.
- **Exercises:** the current modules' formats (open tasks with evidence-based feedback, generated grammar sets, transcription-supported listening, dictation, contextual vocabulary).
- **Progression:** B2 checkpoints, mostly open tasks. AI feedback is evidence and is shown, never an automatic level change.

### C1
- **Communicative objectives:**
  - understand demanding, longer texts and implicit meaning;
  - express ideas fluently and spontaneously without obvious searching;
  - use language flexibly for social, academic and professional purposes;
  - produce clear, well-structured, detailed text on complex subjects.
- **Vocabulary:** about 3,750–4,500 receptive. Nuance, connotation, register shifts, idiomatic and academic vocabulary, collocation density.
- **Grammar:**
  - inversion;
  - cleft sentences;
  - advanced modality and hedging;
  - nominalisation;
  - ellipsis and substitution;
  - advanced participle clauses;
  - emphasis devices.

  These are already modelled in the app's 12 grammar topics.
- **Listening:** extended speech even when not clearly structured; varied accents; implicit relationships.
- **Speaking:** spontaneous, precise, well-organised speech; adapting register.
- **Reading:** long, complex factual and literary texts; specialised articles.
- **Writing:** well-structured complex texts; choosing style for the reader.
- **Exercises:**
  - current modules;
  - register transformation;
  - collocation and sense precision;
  - summarising;
  - argument evaluation.
- **Progression:** C1 checkpoints with rubric-based self and AI-assisted assessment. Human assessment is recommended for productive skills.

### C2
- **Communicative objectives:**
  - understand virtually everything heard or read;
  - summarise and reconstruct arguments from different sources;
  - express themselves spontaneously, very fluently and precisely, differentiating finer shades of meaning in complex situations.
- **Vocabulary:** about 4,500–5,000+ receptive. Idiomatic, colloquial and specialised range, with precise connotation.
- **Grammar:** consistent control of complex language. Errors are rare and slips, not systematic; focus on stylistic choice.
- **Listening:** any spoken language, including fast colloquial speech and specialised lectures.
- **Speaking:** effortless discussion, with rhetorical control.
- **Reading:** abstract, structurally complex or highly colloquial texts.
- **Writing:** complex, stylistically appropriate texts: critiques, reports and summaries from several sources.
- **Exercises:**
  - authentic input (own media, already supported);
  - synthesis from several sources;
  - style and register editing;
  - fine collocation and connotation work.
- **Progression:** a self-directed maintenance mode. The app reports evidence; it does not claim C2.

## 4. The first beginner journey

### 4.1 Stage 0: orientation (in Portuguese)
- **What the app does** and how a lesson works (one minute).
- **The instruction words,** with icons and audio, until the learner can follow English instructions: listen, read, repeat, choose, write, match, check, next, again.
- **First win in the first five minutes:** say "Hello, my name is …".

### 4.2 Unit structure
Every unit is built around **one communicative goal** stated in Portuguese ("No fim desta unidade consegues apresentar-te e perguntar o nome a alguém"). Its steps, about 15–25 minutes per lesson and two to four lessons per unit:
1. **Watch or listen to a model:** a short dialogue with audio, played slowly first, with a Portuguese translation on demand.
2. **Meet the language:**
   - new words and chunks, each with audio, a picture or translation, and an example from the dialogue;
   - recognition first.
3. **Notice the pattern:**
   - **A short explanation in Portuguese,** with English examples from the dialogue.
   - **Contrast with Portuguese where it predicts errors:**
     - "Tenho 20 anos" → "I am 20";
     - the adjective goes before the noun;
     - subjects can't be dropped ("It is raining", not "Is raining").
   - **Minimal terminology,** always explained.
4. **Guided practice, from receptive to productive:**
   - listen and choose → match → order the words → choose the gap → type the gap → answer the question.
   - Immediate feedback that explains *why*, in Portuguese.
5. **Use:**
   - a guided role-play: the learner says lines from options, then freely;
   - a short written message;
   - the same goal as the model dialogue, with the learner's own details.
6. **Review:**
   - spaced retrieval of the unit's items, mixed with earlier units;
   - this is where the Vocabulary Trainer lives for beginners.
7. **Checkpoint:**
   - a short task proving the goal, in all four skills where practical;
   - passing it is the progression signal.

### 4.3 First units (indicative, Pre-A1 → A1)
| # | Goal | Key language | Grammar point |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | Follow the app's instructions | listen, read, repeat, choose… | — |

| 2 | Say where you're from and your age | countries, nationalities, numbers 0–100 | `be`: I/you/he/she, questions and short answers |
| 3 | Talk about your family | family words, possessives | my/your/his/her; 's; `have got` |
| 4 | Name things around you | objects, colours | a/an; plurals; this/that/these/those; `there is/are` |
| 5 | Tell the time and talk about days | time, days, months | prepositions of time (at, on, in), basic |
| 6 | Describe your daily routine | routine verbs, frequency adverbs | present simple I/you/we/they |
| 7 | Ask about someone's routine | — | present simple he/she; do/does questions and negatives |

| 9 | Ask for and follow directions | places, directions | imperatives; prepositions of place |
| 10 | Talk about what you like and can do | hobbies, sports | like + -ing; `can` |
| 11 | Say what is happening now | actions | present continuous |
| 12 | Talk about yesterday | time phrases | was/were; past simple (regular, first irregulars) |

### 4.4 How the skills reinforce each other
- **Shared content:** one unit's words, chunks and grammar point appear in its dialogue (listening), its text (reading), its role-play (speaking) and its message (writing). The Vocabulary Trainer reviews exactly those items. Content records share ids, so a word links to the lines and lessons where it appears.
- **Graded input:** later reading and listening only use words already taught, plus a small number of new ones glossed in place, which keeps the input comprehensible.
- **Mistakes feed review:** mistakes in any skill become observations (the Learning Engine already does this) and are scheduled into review or remedial practice for that point. They do not stay as statistics only.
- **Free practice comes later:** the existing free modules stay available, but at A1 Today recommends the next lesson and the due review first. Free practice is offered once the learner has the level to benefit.

### 4.5 Errors to anticipate for Portuguese speakers
Use these for explanations and distractors. Swan & Smith, *Learner English* (2nd edition, 2001) has a chapter on Portuguese speakers worth consulting.
- **Grammar:**
  - age with `have` ("I have 20 years");
  - dropped subjects ("Is raining");
  - adjective order;
  - article with general nouns ("The life is hard");
  - missing do-support ("You like coffee?");
  - "people is";
  - "make" vs "do".
- **False friends:**
  - actually ≠ atualmente;
  - pretend ≠ pretender;
  - library ≠ livraria;
  - push ≠ puxar;
  - eventually ≠ eventualmente.
- **Pronunciation:**
  - th;
  - h;
  - vowel length (ship/sheep);
  - final -ed;
  - epenthetic vowels ("espeak");
  - final consonants;
  - word stress.

## 5. Vocabulary training by level

| Band | Exercise sequence | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pre-A1 / A1 | hear and see → recognise (picture, audio, translation options) → order and choose → type only after recognition is stable | Audio is required. Portuguese translations are the main meaning support. No English definitions. |
| A2 / B1 | recognition → recall (Portuguese → English typed) → context (gap with exact form) → word forms → sentence production | Definitions in simple English from B1. First collocations and phrasal verbs. |
| B2 / C1 / C2 | context with forms → collocations → multiple senses → definition → register and synonyms → production in open tasks | The current trainer fits here. Add sense discrimination and collocation exercises. |

Pronunciation practice starts with listen-and-repeat with self-comparison (record, then compare with the model). Automatic pronunciation *scoring* waits for a reliable method; it is not faked.

## 6. Methodology and evidence of learning

### 6.1 Methods mapped to the design
| Method | How it appears |
| --- | --- |
| Spaced repetition | Per item (sense, chunk, grammar point) and per direction. The schedule adapts to item difficulty. The current fixed ladder is a starting point; an adaptive open algorithm (e.g. FSRS) can be evaluated later. |
| Retrieval practice | Every lesson's practice and every review asks the learner to retrieve, not re-read. |
| Interleaving | Reviews mix items from several units and types. Checkpoints mix skills. |
| Comprehensible input | Graded texts and audio built from taught items (§1.5). |
| Progressive difficulty | Within a lesson (receptive → productive) and across units (path). |
| Error-based learning | Errors become observations, explanations and scheduled remediation, as the engine already does for B2+, extended to beginner categories. |
| Explicit grammar | Short explanations in the support language before practice, with contrast to Portuguese. |
| Receptive vs productive | Separate states and exercises (§6.2). |
| Adaptive learning | Placement per skill. The path skips what the learner shows they know. Review intensity follows retention. |

### 6.2 Knowledge states
Per item and direction:

`unseen → met → recognised → recalled → used`

- **met:** presented in a lesson.
- **recognised:** chosen correctly among options, or understood by ear, at least a day after first meeting it.
- **recalled:** produced from the meaning (typed or spoken) after a delay.
- **used:** produced correctly in an open task (speaking or writing), with evidence.

States can go down after failed retrieval. Each state change keeps the observation that caused it, consistent with the engine's traceability rule.

### 6.3 Separating activity volume from learning
- **Primary progress indicators:**
  - checkpoints passed per skill;
  - delayed retention (the share of items recalled correctly at least 7 days after the previous success);
  - items per state;
  - accuracy trends per category with their denominators.
- **Secondary context only,** clearly labelled as effort and never as progress:
  - activity (minutes, actions, active days).
- **No streaks, points or badges** (an existing project rule).
- **Rule:** a metric that can go up without the learner knowing more is not a progress metric.
